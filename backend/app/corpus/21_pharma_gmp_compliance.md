Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) regulations govern pharmaceutical manufacturing to ensure products are consistently produced and controlled to quality standards appropriate for their intended use. In the United States, 21 CFR Parts 210 and 211 establish GMP requirements for finished pharmaceuticals; Part 820 governs medical devices. The ICH Q7 guideline extends GMP to active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) manufacturing. Non-compliance with GMP can result in product recalls, FDA warning letters, import bans, and criminal liability.

Electronic Batch Records (EBR) replace paper batch records with digital documentation systems that capture manufacturing instructions, operator actions, equipment data, and quality results in a structured, auditable format. EBR systems (Veeva Vault MFG, Apprentice, Werum PAS-X) enforce correct-by-construction process execution: operators cannot proceed to the next step until required actions are documented, out-of-specification results are investigated, and electronic signatures are obtained from qualified personnel.

21 CFR Part 11 requires that electronic records and electronic signatures used in regulated activities meet specific requirements: audit trails capturing who changed what and when, access controls, system validation documentation, and signature manifestations. Part 11 compliance is prerequisite for FDA acceptance of electronic records during inspections and audits.

The annual product review (APR) or product quality review (PQR), required by GMP regulations, analyzes all batches manufactured in the review period for trends in yield, reject rates, out-of-specification results, and deviations. Automated APR generation from electronic batch records and LIMS (Laboratory Information Management System) data reduces APR preparation time from weeks to days while improving completeness and accuracy.

Manufacturing deviation management — documenting, investigating, and resolving departures from approved procedures — is a high-volume, documentation-intensive process in pharmaceutical manufacturing. CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) systems manage the investigation and resolution of deviations, OOS results, complaints, and audit findings. AI assistance in deviation classification, similar deviation identification, and root cause suggestion can reduce investigation time while maintaining GMP documentation standards.

Human oversight and qualified person (QP) sign-off is an absolute regulatory requirement for pharmaceutical batch release — automation cannot substitute for the legal responsibility of a qualified person reviewing and releasing each batch. AI tools can assist the QP with data compilation and exception flagging, but the release decision is always human-made.
