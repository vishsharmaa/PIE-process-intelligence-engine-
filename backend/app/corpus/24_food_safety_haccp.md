Food safety management in manufacturing is governed by HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points), a systematic preventive approach to food safety that identifies physical, chemical, and biological hazards in production processes and establishes critical control points (CCPs) where controls are essential to prevent, eliminate, or reduce hazards to acceptable levels. HACCP is required by FDA's Preventive Controls for Human Food rule (21 CFR Part 117) and is the international standard under Codex Alimentarius.

The seven HACCP principles are: (1) Conduct hazard analysis; (2) Identify critical control points; (3) Establish critical limits; (4) Establish monitoring procedures; (5) Establish corrective actions; (6) Establish verification procedures; (7) Establish record-keeping and documentation. Critical limits — maximum or minimum values (temperature, pH, water activity, chlorine concentration) that must be controlled to prevent, eliminate, or reduce hazard occurrence — are scientifically established for each CCP.

Automated monitoring systems at CCPs provide continuous data capture and automatic alarm triggering when critical limits are approached or exceeded. Thermal processing (pasteurization, sterilization) is the most rigorously monitored CCP: temperature sensors with 1-second scan rates, SCADA-driven process control, and continuous chart recorder data provide the regulatory documentation required to prove adequate pathogen kill. Metal detection and X-ray inspection at end-of-line detect physical contamination at throughputs of 200–400 units per minute.

Allergen management — preventing cross-contact between allergen-containing and allergen-free products — requires documented cleaning and changeover procedures, dedicated equipment where feasible, and allergen testing of finished products and environmental swabs. The Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act (FALCPA) requires accurate labeling of the nine major allergens; mislabeled allergens are among the leading causes of food recalls.

Digital food safety management systems (SafetyChain, TraceGains, ComplianceMate) automate HACCP monitoring record-keeping, CCP deviation documentation, corrective action tracking, and regulatory reporting. Integration with production equipment enables automated record creation, eliminating manual data entry errors. However, food safety decisions — particularly in novel situations, allergen control, and regulatory interpretation — require qualified food safety professionals, not automation alone.
