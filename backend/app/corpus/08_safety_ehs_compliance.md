Environment, Health, and Safety (EHS) compliance management encompasses the processes by which manufacturing organizations meet regulatory requirements, prevent workplace injuries and illnesses, and minimize environmental impact. EHS is among the most heavily regulated domains in industrial operations, with compliance obligations under OSHA (Occupational Safety and Health Administration), EPA (Environmental Protection Agency), and dozens of industry-specific standards.

OSHA recordkeeping requirements mandate that employers track work-related injuries and illnesses on OSHA 300 logs, 300A summaries, and 301 incident reports. Establishments in high-hazard industries must electronically submit injury and illness data to OSHA annually. The OSHA recordable incident rate (RIR) is the primary industry safety KPI: RIR = (number of recordable incidents × 200,000) / employee-hours worked. Manufacturing industry average RIR is approximately 3.0; best-in-class facilities target below 1.0.

Process Safety Management (PSM) under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119 applies to facilities handling highly hazardous chemicals above threshold quantities. PSM requires Process Hazard Analysis (PHA/HAZOP), written operating procedures, mechanical integrity programs, management of change (MOC) procedures, incident investigation, and emergency action planning. PSM audits by OSHA inspectors focus on documentation completeness and procedural compliance.

Environmental compliance includes air emissions reporting under the Clean Air Act (Title V operating permits, HAP emissions tracking), wastewater discharge permits (NPDES), hazardous waste manifests and disposal records (RCRA), and chemical inventory reporting under EPCRA (Emergency Planning and Community Right-to-Know Act) Tier II and TRI (Toxic Release Inventory) submissions.

Digital EHS management systems automate compliance tracking, incident reporting, corrective action management, audit scheduling, and regulatory submission workflows. EHSQ software vendors including Intelex, Cority, Enablon, and SAP EHS provide cloud platforms that consolidate compliance data, generate automated regulatory reports, and provide real-time dashboards for safety performance metrics.

Human judgment remains essential in EHS processes: incident root cause analysis requires investigator expertise; HAZOP facilitation requires process engineering knowledge; and regulatory interpretation in novel situations requires legal and technical judgment. AI can assist with data collection, trend analysis, and compliance calendar management, but final safety decisions require qualified human sign-off.
