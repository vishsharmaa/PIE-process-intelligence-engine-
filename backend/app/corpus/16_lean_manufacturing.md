Lean manufacturing eliminates waste (muda) from production processes to maximize value delivery to customers. The seven classic wastes are: Overproduction, Waiting, Transportation, Over-processing, Inventory, Motion, and Defects (TIMWOOD). Value Stream Mapping (VSM) visualizes the flow of materials and information from raw material to customer, identifying waste and improvement opportunities.

Takt time — the rate at which products must be completed to meet customer demand — is the fundamental pacing mechanism of lean production. Takt time = Available production time / Customer demand. Balancing work elements to takt time ensures smooth flow without overproduction or bottlenecks. Single-Minute Exchange of Die (SMED) reduces changeover time to under 10 minutes through standardization, parallel activities, and dedicated tooling.

5S workplace organization (Sort, Set in order, Shine, Standardize, Sustain) creates visual workplaces where abnormalities are immediately apparent. Kanban systems use physical or electronic signals to trigger replenishment, ensuring pull-based production flow aligned with actual consumption. Total Productive Maintenance (TPM) engages production operators in equipment care, reducing breakdowns and improving OEE.

Digital lean tools — electronic kanban, digital andon systems, real-time OEE dashboards — extend lean principles with data visibility. However, lean fundamentally requires engaged employees who understand and practice waste elimination daily. AI can surface improvement opportunities and track KPIs, but the improvement work itself is human-led.
