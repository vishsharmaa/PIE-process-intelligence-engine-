Reliability-Centered Maintenance (RCM) provides a structured framework for selecting the optimal maintenance strategy for each failure mode. RCM analysis identifies the functions of each asset, the ways in which those functions can fail, the consequences of each failure, and the most cost-effective maintenance strategy to prevent or mitigate those consequences.

Failure modes are catalogued through Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA), which assesses each failure mode by its Severity (1–10), Occurrence probability (1–10), and Detectability (1–10). The Risk Priority Number (RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detectability) guides prioritization. High-RPN failure modes with detectable precursors are the best candidates for predictive maintenance.

Statistical models for failure prediction include Weibull analysis for life data modeling, Proportional Hazard Models for covariate-adjusted survival analysis, and Hidden Markov Models for degradation state estimation. Weibull shape parameters indicate failure patterns: β < 1 indicates infant mortality, β ≈ 1 indicates random failures (exponential distribution), and β > 1 indicates wear-out failures amenable to condition monitoring.

Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) and Mean Time To Repair (MTTR) are the primary KPIs for maintenance program effectiveness. Industry benchmarks vary significantly: rotating equipment in process industries averages MTBF of 2–5 years; electrical systems average 10–15 years; mechanical fasteners and consumables average weeks to months. Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) combines availability, performance, and quality metrics into a single operational efficiency measure.

Predictive maintenance is most cost-effective when: the asset has a high replacement cost or long lead time for spares; the failure mode produces detectable precursor signals; the cost of inspection is low relative to the cost of failure; and unplanned failure consequences include safety risk, quality impact, or significant production loss. Conversely, run-to-failure (reactive maintenance) is appropriate for cheap, easily replaceable assets with no detectable failure progression.
