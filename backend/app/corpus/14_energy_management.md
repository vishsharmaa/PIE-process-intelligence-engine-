Industrial energy management encompasses monitoring, analyzing, and optimizing energy consumption across manufacturing facilities. Energy typically represents 10–40% of total manufacturing cost in energy-intensive industries (aluminum, cement, chemicals, glass, pulp and paper), making energy efficiency a major profitability lever. ISO 50001 provides the international standard framework for Energy Management Systems (EnMS), analogous to ISO 9001 for quality management.

Automated energy monitoring systems collect real-time consumption data from electrical sub-metering, gas flow meters, steam flow meters, and compressed air flow meters. Energy data historians — often integrated with process historians — enable correlation of energy consumption with production volume, product mix, ambient temperature, and equipment operating state. Automated energy dashboards provide real-time Energy Intensity (energy per unit of production) versus baseline, alerting operators to inefficiencies as they occur.

Machine learning models trained on energy consumption patterns can predict consumption for production planning (enabling demand response participation), detect anomalies indicative of equipment degradation or inefficiency (leaking compressed air, worn motor bearings, heat exchanger fouling), and optimize setpoints for major energy consumers (boilers, chillers, HVAC, compressed air systems) to minimize energy cost while meeting process requirements.

Compressed air systems account for 10% of industrial electricity consumption in the U.S. and are notoriously inefficient, with typical system efficiency of 10–15% (electrical to useful pneumatic work). Automated leak detection using ultrasonic sensors, pressure monitoring, and flow analysis can identify and quantify leaks; automated repair prioritization based on leak severity reduces compressed air energy waste by 20–30% in most facilities.

Demand response programs — utility-offered incentives for industrial customers to reduce consumption during peak demand periods — provide revenue streams for manufacturers with flexible loads. Automated demand response systems integrate utility price signals with production schedules, enabling automated shedding of non-critical loads (HVAC, lighting, non-urgent production) within 10 minutes of a demand response event.

ISO 50001 certification requires a documented energy policy, energy review, energy performance indicators, energy baseline, energy objectives and targets, action plans, and management review. Organizations that have implemented ISO 50001 report average energy cost savings of 10% in the first year of certification.
